Paver Restoration

Patios, driveways and walkways brought back to the color they were the day they were laid — then locked in with polymeric sand and a sealer that keeps them that way.

Get a free estimate 609-379-1760

Pavers do not fail all at once. The sand washes out of the joints, weeds and ants move into the gap, the surface goes grey and blotchy, and a patio that cost thousands starts to look like a liability. Restoration reverses all of that in one visit.

This is the service we are known for. A full paver restoration is three jobs in sequence, and skipping any one of them is why so many patios look good for a month and then go right back.

What is included

  • Deep clean that lifts embedded dirt, algae and weeds
  • Joints refilled with polymeric sand to lock pavers in place
  • Sealer applied to hold color and block future staining
  • Rust, oil and efflorescence treated before sealing

Not sure this is what you need? Call 609-379-1760 and describe what you are looking at. If a different service is the right answer — or if it genuinely does not need doing yet — we will tell you that.

How we do it

  1. Deep clean. Surface cleaner and hot water lift years of dirt, algae and organic growth out of the paver face and the joints. Weeds and moss come out with it.
  2. Treat the stains. Rust, oil, efflorescence and leaf tannin need chemistry, not pressure. Each gets the right treatment before anything is sealed over it.
  3. Re-sand the joints. Fresh polymeric sand is swept into every joint and set. That is what stops shifting, stops weeds and stops the ant hills.
  4. Seal. A sealer suited to your paver and the look you want — natural, matte or wet-look. It holds the color and makes the next cleaning far easier.

Why it matters

Stops the shifting

Empty joints let pavers move, and moving pavers become trip hazards and low spots that hold water. Polymeric sand solves it.

Keeps weeds out

Weeds do not grow out of pavers, they grow out of the sand between them. Fill the joints properly and the problem goes away.

Protects the money already spent

A sealed paver surface resists oil, rust and salt. It is the cheapest thing you can do to avoid replacing the patio.

Common questions

How long does a paver restoration take?

Most residential patios and driveways are a one- or two-day job. Cleaning and stain treatment happen first, then the surface has to dry fully before sand and sealer go down — which is why we watch the forecast before we schedule you.

How often should pavers be sealed?

Typically every three to five years, depending on sun exposure, traffic and the sealer used. We will tell you honestly if yours do not need it yet.

Will sealing change how my pavers look?

That is your call. Natural-look sealers keep the current appearance and just protect it. Wet-look sealers darken and enrich the color. We will show you both on your own pavers before we commit.

Can you get rust and oil stains out?

Usually, yes. Rust from irrigation and fertilizer and oil from vehicles both come out with the right treatment. Very old, deeply set stains may lighten rather than vanish — we will tell you which you are dealing with before you pay for anything.
